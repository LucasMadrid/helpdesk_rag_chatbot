# Working Hours and Overtime

This page explains how working time is organised at Corvane, when extra hours count as overtime and how overtime is compensated. It applies to all employees in Dublin, Lisbon and fully remote roles. Where your employment contract or local law gives you a better arrangement, that arrangement applies.

## Working hours

The standard full-time working week at Corvane is 39 hours, normally worked Monday to Friday. Part-time employees work the hours stated in their contract, and everything on this page applies to them pro rata.

Within the week you have flexibility over when you start and finish, as long as you are available during core hours, 10:00 to 16:00 local time. Lisbon employees use Lisbon time, and fully remote employees use the time zone of the office their contract is linked to. Meetings with colleagues in the other office should be scheduled within the core hours of both locations whenever possible.

Take a break of at least 30 minutes for every day on which you work more than six hours. Breaks are not counted as working time. You should also have at least 11 consecutive hours of rest between the end of one working day and the start of the next.

The table below summarises the main limits.

| Rule | Limit | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Standard working week | 39 hours | All full-time employees |
| Core hours | 10:00 to 16:00 local time | All employees, Monday to Friday |
| Minimum break | 30 minutes per day over 6 hours worked | All employees |
| Daily rest | 11 consecutive hours | All employees |
| Average weekly maximum | 48 hours including overtime, averaged over 4 months | All employees |

## Overtime

Overtime is time worked beyond your contractual weekly hours at your manager's request or with your manager's approval. Overtime must be approved in advance. Hours worked without prior approval do not count as overtime, unless they were needed to deal with a P1 incident and your manager confirms them in writing on the next working day.

To request approval, ask your manager before the extra work starts, ideally in writing, and explain the reason and the expected number of hours. Planned overtime, for example for a weekend system migration or a customer go-live, should be agreed at least one week ahead.

Record all approved overtime hours in Orbit HR under Time, within the same week in which they were worked. Your manager approves the entry in Orbit HR. Overtime entries that have not been approved by the payroll cut-off on the 10th of the month are processed in the following month.

Managers are responsible for keeping overtime the exception rather than the rule. If a team regularly needs overtime to complete its work, the manager should raise this with People Operations so that the workload or staffing can be reviewed.

## Compensation for overtime

Approved overtime is compensated with time off, not with extra pay, unless your contract says otherwise. For every 8 hours of approved overtime you earn 1 compensation day: a full day off that you can take at a time agreed with your manager, in the same way as annual leave. Overtime hours are added up across the year, so they do not need to be worked in a single block. Hours that do not yet add up to 8 stay on your balance until they do.

You can earn a maximum of 10 compensation days per calendar year. Overtime beyond that limit is not converted into further compensation days, so talk to your manager early if you expect to exceed it. Compensation days should be used within the calendar year in which they were earned, and any that remain on 31 December expire unless your manager and People Operations agree otherwise in writing.

Request a compensation day in Orbit HR using the "Compensation day (overtime)" leave type. Your balance of overtime hours and earned compensation days is shown on your Time page.

Please note that the term compensation day is also used in hr-leave-policy for the day off you receive when a company holiday falls on a weekend. That is a separate entitlement with its own rules and does not count towards the limit of 10 described here.
